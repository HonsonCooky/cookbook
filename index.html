About

Software engineering seems a strange place to end up, coming from what friends would jest is a singing cult. I spent years on stage alongside friends and family, thoroughly enjoying being a unique part in a larger production. It was ultimately a knack for calculus and a strange draw toward technology that landed me here.

Watching my first program run in my late teens felt like magic. It was a moment that hooked me into completing a Bachelor of Engineering at Victoria University. Four years of late nights and mild caffeine abuse rewired my brain to make sense of it all. I threw myself into it completely, walking out with First Class Honours in Software Engineering and a Specialisation in Networked Applications. They remain some of the most difficult years of my engineering journey thus far, but also the ones I would most readily repeat. I emerged relieved, satiated, and somehow still hungry for more.

Without a bar's rest, I stepped into the industry. My career has spanned a wide variety of environments, giving me a front-row seat to how software delivers value, and the multitude of difficulties that come with it. In my view, the toughest battles are much like a stage production. A definitive output always has to be delivered, but unless you are a one-man show, some shared vision of the future is the only thing that aligns everyone to pull something extraordinary together.

Because I'm naturally drawn to this craft, it tends to spill over into my free time as well. Like a musician, I practice - exploring, experimenting, and reinforcing. This has taken many forms: converting to Neovim for some time, building desktop computers for myself and others, or adapting to ergonomic keyboards. I'm currently developing 3D video games with Odin, exploring my own workflow that avoids the noise of modern tooling. It is all just quiet practice, refining my own approach to the craft.

Interests

Most of what I do in my own time isn't separate from my work - it's the same curiosity pointed in different directions. I've written about how I tend to think in Wired to Ask; what follows is where that goes when I pick the problem myself.

Game Development from Scratch

I am currently developing 3D video games from first principles using Odin. Rather than relying on heavy mainstream engines, I am exploring how to architect game systems from the ground up. It is less about shipping a commercial title and more about finding my own zen-crafting a highly personal, quiet workflow away from the noise of modern tooling.

Hardware & Ergonomics

I maintain a steady interest in modern commercial hardware. This initially started as a purely practical pursuit: building my own computer and hunting down the exact ergonomic keyboard I needed to be an effective developer. With those physical bottlenecks now solved, I just keep a finger on the pulse of the space, ready to adapt if my workflows ever need to shift.

Health & Fitness

I view physical health largely through the lens of cognitive longevity. Every body is different, but I have found that to consistently get the most out of my brain every single day, I require a solid baseline of diet, exercise, and routine. I don't chase fitness records; I strictly chase the energy required for peak daily focus.

Linux

While I am currently bound to Windows for practical reasons, I would love to eventually make a setup like Omarchy my daily driver. In truth, whenever life starts feeling a little too predictable, I will format a drive, install a new Linux distribution, and spend a weekend tinkering just for the fun of it.
