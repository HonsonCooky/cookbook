About

Software engineering seems a strange place to end up, coming from what some would jest is a singing cult. Years on stage alongside friends and family left a mark I have never shaken: a desire to contribute to productions only made possible because of every person's contributions. Ultimately, it was a knack for calculus and a quiet fascination with technology that sent me down a different path.

Watching my first program run in my late teens felt like magic. It was a moment that hooked me into completing a Bachelor of Engineering at Victoria University. Four years of late nights and mild caffeine abuse rewired my brain to make sense of it all. I threw myself into it completely, walking out with First Class Honours in Software Engineering and a Specialisation in Networked Applications. They remain some of the most difficult years of my engineering journey thus far, but also the ones I would most readily repeat. I emerged relieved, satiated, and somehow still hungry for more.

Without a bar's rest, I stepped into the industry. My career has spanned a wide variety of environments, giving me a front-row seat to how software delivers value, and the multitude of difficulties that come with it. In my view, the toughest battles are much like a stage production. A definitive output always has to be delivered, but unless you are a one-man show, some shared vision of the future is the only thing that aligns the uncountable number of variables needed to output something extraordinary.

Being naturally drawn to this craft and its potential, it tends to spill over into my free time as well. Like a musician, I practice: exploring, experimenting, and reinforcing. Exploring Vim motions, building desktop computers, adapting to ergonomic keyboards and layouts. Currently, I'm developing 3D video games in Odin, exploring my own workflow that avoids the noise of modern tooling. It is all just quiet practice for myself, refining my own approach to this craft.

Interests

Every day, I try to make small improvements toward the best version of myself. An endless battle, that I casually take on. In this pursuit, I've come to explore the following topics.

Programmatic Game Development

Growing up on PS2 games left a lasting impression. I'm also still surrounded by friends and family who write stories and record music simply for the love of their craft. Sitting among their creative outputs makes me want to bring it to life, which I slowly chip away at on the side. I don't really view myself as a typical indie game developer; I'm a software engineer who enjoys the underlying mechanics of building a game. Instead of learning heavy engines, I prefer a purely programmatic approach-spending a quiet Sunday morning figuring out how to render a .glb model in Odin.

Effective Workflows

I like finding ways to make my daily environment more efficient. That has meant spending time in Neovim, trying out keyboard-only workflows, and adopting ergonomic setups - from a Planck to my current ZSA Voyager. The aim is to find the operational model that costs the least amount of brain energy. I'm doing a lot less exploring in this space now, having found most of my foundations.

Hardware

My degree taught me to understand software from the hardware up, and that interest stuck around. I keep an eye on new tech releases and enjoy the practical side of building and maintaining my own setups. It's a nice, tangible change of pace from writing software. In the world of computing, I like being able to build it all.
