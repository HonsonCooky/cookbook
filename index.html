About

Software engineering is a strange place to end up after a childhood spent in a musical household. My friends would often jest that I was being raised in a singing cult. Years of performing on stage formed the foundation of my energetic personality. Meanwhile, a hidden knack for calculus and a fascination with computers landed me in a digital technologies class during my late teens. It was a rather boring and ordinary school day where I was introduced to the wonderful perplexities of software development; a world that felt extraordinarily similar to magic.

Being of the what are you going to do with your life age, I pursued further education to really master this magic, which eventuated in my Bachelor of Engineering. It took four years of late nights, mild caffeine abuse and living on the bare essentials, to reforge the foundational neurological wiring of my brain to make sense of it all. I emerged relieved, but satiated, and hungry for more.

Following university, I moved into corporate software engineering: a domain where programming computers meets economic variables. It has taken some time to really understand how to deliver software in this space, because modern software development is far more akin to an art form than its etymology might suggest. Success here is almost always about how well defined our shared vision of the future is. Coming together on building a future world that doesn't currently exist. What programming language to use, which AI tools to adopt, and even system designs, emerge almost naturally toward that ultimate goal.

Outside of work, I enjoy exploring this space in a more relaxed and casual manner: programming, experimenting, and often producing just enough to understand before moving on. And what do I have to show for it? Not much. But I strive to be a better engineer every day, and this floundering and foraging, this winding and weaving through different ideas and technologies, is how I do that. Along the way I've discovered and rediscovered critical ideas. They sum up to who I am, and to a continued journey through a world so many don't realise exists right below their fingertips.

Interests

Most of what I do in my own time isn't separate from my work - it's the same curiosity pointed in different directions. I've written about how I tend to think in Wired to Ask; what follows is where that goes when I pick the problem myself.

Game Engines

I'm building my own game engine, less to replace what exists than to change the workflow around it. Mainstream engines are tightly coupled to their own way of working, which is what lets them scale, but it also asks for more surface knowledge than I've been good at carrying. Building from the ground up, with AI in the loop, is how I look for a smaller surface area - one that fits how I think.

Hardware

I've built my own PC, and the part that stuck wasn't the spec sheet but a rough intuition for what modern hardware can do. Something there doesn't add up to me: today's machines are well beyond what last decade's games needed, yet current titles still bring strong hardware to its knees. My guess is that a lot of the effort goes into low-level optimisation while higher-level wins go untouched - though I'm reasoning from the outside, and the people shipping these games are working against constraints I can't see.

Ergo Keyboards

This one began as a fix rather than a hobby. A childhood elbow injury left my right wrist with limited rotation - a non-issue at school, until full-time keyboard work turned it into a real one. Solving it pulled me into ergonomic boards, through a Planck EZ and a Glove80 and a fair amount of trial and error, and landed me on a ZSA Voyager, which has been my keyboard ever since. Being able to rest my elbow properly again made a real difference, and I've stayed interested in the space.

Linux

I run Linux as my daily OS where the workflow suits it, and move between Linux and Windows depending on what the task needs. The control is the part I enjoy; switching when it's the easier path is just pragmatic.

The Outdoors

Getting outdoors, hiking most often, is where ideas tend to surface for me. Stepping away from the screen gives them room to connect in ways they don't while I'm staring at the problem. It looks like time off, and partly is, but it's also where a lot of the thinking happens.
